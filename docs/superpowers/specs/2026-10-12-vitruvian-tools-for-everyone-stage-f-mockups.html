Tools for everyone, stage F: mock-ups

Vitruvian for macOS. The Nexus Agent feature becomes the Nexus Agent tool, and HomeSpeaker gains a tool beside it: pictures of what changes, before any code.

100%

Two tools, one after the other, the way stage E moved the GitHub feature. Nexus Agent: the app's feature, 7,500 lines on the shared engine, becomes a tool of protocol 2 in the standalone app's own package (apps/desktop/nexus-agent/macos), released from the standalone's mirror beside the app, listed as "Nexus Agent" by VitruvianSoftware; its Quick Prompt is a window of the tool's own process, its island page a tile, its settings panel the bot's. In 3.48 the feature's page says so and hands the tool its settings when the tool is installed; 3.49 removes the feature. HomeSpeaker: already an app of its own and no feature of Vitruvian's, it gains a tool beside it, in its own folder and its own release, that speaks through HomeSpeaker's program and edits the settings file both share. Every word inside a panel is the tool's own; the app's one new text is in the table at the end, word for word.

Six kinds of block: heading, button (Open the Quick Prompt, Start or Stop), text (the agent's state, the bot's problem in the feature's own sentence), divider, row (the bot) and list (the sessions, at most ten, titles cut to 80 characters). The page is drawn again (ui/render) when the bot starts or stops, a reply begins or ends, or a turn stops for approval. The words are the tool's, in the fifteen languages the feature had.

Choice: the chat itself does not go into the island. A block cannot hold a streaming conversation, and the design keeps the Quick Prompt as the tool's own window; the island page is the way to it and the glance at it.

Alternative: a web panel with a chat page. It would be a second chat to keep beside the shared SwiftUI one, in a web view, for one page.

Four of the tool's eight preferences have a title, so the app draws them as it draws any tool's settings, in the person's language: the bot folder, starting the bot with the app, plan mode and worktree mode, the ones the feature's page and chat had. The four without a title, the provider chosen, the person's own providers, the prompt history and the archived sessions, are handed over once and never shown. Under them the tool's settings panel is the feature's page without those four: the bot's state and log, and the part of its .env a person changes, with Save enabled only when something changed.

Choice: approval mode and reasoning effort are lists of rows with a check mark, because a panel has no picker (stage E's gap 1). A word in the file the page has no name for (max) is a row of its own, as the feature's page showed it.

Alternative: the four titled settings inside the panel too. Fields the app draws are the same controls, need no page from the tool, and the app's copy of plan and worktree mode follows the chat through storage/set.

Seven kinds of block: heading, field (what to say, submitted with Return; the volume, a number), text (where it was heard, or why it was not, in HomeSpeaker's own words), toggle (broadcasting and quiet hours, the two switches the phone's module has), row (the speaker and its volume), button (Mute or Unmute, Read the volume), list (the speakers HomeSpeaker found, a tap picks the default), and link when HomeSpeaker is missing. "Say something" runs HomeSpeaker --say, which speaks with the person's sign-in and default speaker and overrides quiet hours, as typing in HomeSpeaker's own box does; the volume goes through --volume, --set-volume, --mute and --unmute, the contract the Mac agent already uses.

Choice: the tool drives HomeSpeaker's program and edits the file both share, rather than linking HomeSpeaker's Google client. One sign-in, one keeper of the secrets, and the menu bar app follows a change within a second, as it follows the phone's.

Alternative: a tool that talks to Google itself. It would need its own sign-in and its own copy of the tokens, and HomeSpeaker's page would not know what it did.
